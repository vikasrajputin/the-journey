Chapter 1 · 13.8 billion years ago

Before Everything

Simulation: the hot, dense early universe

13.8 billion years ago, everything we can see was packed into a space hotter and denser than anything on Earth today.

It was not an explosion into space. Space itself began to stretch, and it is stretching still.

For 380,000 years the universe was a glowing fog. Then it cooled, and light could travel freely. We can still detect that light today.

Gravity pulled gas together into the first stars and galaxies. Touch the sky to light a star.

Stars are furnaces. They forged the carbon, oxygen and iron that would one day make planets, and people.

Look in any direction with a big enough telescope, and you see galaxies. Almost every speck of light here is one.

Chapter 2 · 4.6 billion years ago

A Planet Is Born

Simulation: the young Solar System

4.6 billion years ago, a young Sun was circled by a disc of gas and dust.

Dust stuck to dust. Pebbles became boulders, and boulders became planets.

A Mars-sized world probably struck the young Earth. The debris most likely became our Moon. Tap to strike.

The surface was an ocean of molten rock. As it cooled, a crust formed, and rain filled the first oceans.

Four and a half billion years later, this is what it became.

Chapter 3 · At least 3.5 billion years ago

The First Breath of Life

Real photo: a black smoker vent on the Atlantic sea floor (NOAA)

At least 3.5 billion years ago, life began. One idea is that it started near hot vents on the deep sea floor, like this one.

The first living things were single cells. Touch the water, and watch them divide.

Microbes built layered mounds called stromatolites. In Shark Bay, Australia, they are still being built today.

Microbes that lived on sunlight released oxygen. Slowly, they changed the air of the whole planet.

Chapter 5 · 252 to 66 million years ago

The Age of Giants

Simulation: a Mesozoic forest

For about 165 million years, dinosaurs lived on every continent.

This is Sue, one of the most complete Tyrannosaurus rex skeletons ever found. She was about 12 metres long.

Nobody has ever heard a dinosaur. Their closest relatives, birds and crocodiles, suggest some boomed with closed mouths. Tap to hear a reconstruction.

Then an asteroid about 10 to 15 kilometres wide struck what is now Mexico.

Fire, darkness and cold followed. About three in every four kinds of animals died out.

But not every dinosaur. Birds are the dinosaurs that survived.

Chapter 10 · 14 February 1990

And Then There Was You

Real photo: the Pale Blue Dot, Voyager 1 (NASA/JPL)

Voyager 1 took this photograph from the edge of the Solar System, about 6 billion kilometres from the Sun. That tiny dot in the sunbeam is Earth.

Every person who has ever lived, lived there.

The calcium in your bones and the iron in your blood were made inside stars that died long before the Sun was born.

13.8 billion years. And then there was you.
