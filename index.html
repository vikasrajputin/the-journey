Level 1 · 13.8 billion years ago

The Big Bang

Scientists think the universe is about 13.8 billion years old. They worked this out by studying very old light that still fills space.

At the start, everything was squeezed together and incredibly hot. We call this the Big Bang.

The name is a bit misleading. It was not like a bomb going off in empty space. Space itself started to stretch, everywhere at once.

What came before the Big Bang, if anything? Nobody knows yet. It is one of the biggest open questions in science.

The 3D scene in this level is a simulation made for the game. It shows the idea, not a real photo or an exact model.

The game needs JavaScript. Here is what happens in Chapter 1, level by level.

  1. Level 1: The Big Bang (13.8 billion years ago). Everything in the universe was once packed into a tiny, super-hot speck. Then space began to stretch.
  2. Level 2: Space Stretches (From the start until today). Space is still stretching today. Far-away galaxies are moving away from us, and their light looks redder.
  3. Level 3: The First Building Blocks (The first few minutes). In the first few minutes, tiny particles stuck together to make the centres of hydrogen and helium.
  4. Level 4: The Fog Clears (380,000 years after the Big Bang). When the universe cooled enough, atoms formed, the fog cleared, and light could travel freely for the first time.
  5. Level 5: The First Stars (A few hundred million years after the Big Bang). After a long dark time, gravity squeezed clouds of gas so hard that they lit up. The first stars were born!
  6. Level 6: Star Factory (Inside stars, for billions of years). Stars cook new elements. Big stars explode and scatter them, ready to build planets, and one day, you.
  7. Level 7: Galaxy Builder (The first billion years and beyond). Stars gathered into galaxies, and galaxies crashed and merged to grow bigger. Our home galaxy is the Milky Way.
