Reading the scale

How this scale works

The “All of time” view gives five stretches of time the same width. Inside each one, time runs evenly. The other views use a true scale for a shorter span, so you can see how crowded the recent past really is. Dates count back from the year 2000, a common convention in dating science.

Cosmic time

  1. About 13.8 billion years ago · Cosmic

    The universe begins expanding from a hot, dense state

    The earliest state described by the Big Bang model. What came before, if anything, is unknown.

    ●●●Well establishedChapter 1: Before Everything

  2. Within the first few minutes · Cosmic

    Hydrogen and helium nuclei form

    Fusion in the cooling early universe produces the nuclei of the lightest elements.

    ●●○Strong evidenceChapter 1: Before Everything

  3. About 380,000 years after the Big Bang · Cosmic

    The universe becomes transparent

    Neutral atoms form and light travels freely; we detect this light today as the cosmic microwave background.

    ●●●Well establishedChapter 1: Before Everything

  4. Within a few hundred million years of the Big Bang · Cosmic

    The first stars and galaxies

    Gravity gathers gas into the first stars, which begin forging heavier elements.

    ●○○Active researchChapter 1: Before Everything

  5. About 4.6 billion years ago · Earth

    The Solar System forms

    A collapsing cloud of gas and dust forms the Sun and a disc from which the planets grow.

    ●●●Well establishedChapter 2: A Planet Is Born

Early Earth and microbial life

  1. About 4.54 billion years ago · Earth

    Earth forms

    Earth grows by accretion; radiometric dating gives an age of about 4.54 billion years, ±1%.

    ●●●Well establishedChapter 2: A Planet Is Born

  2. About 4.5 billion years ago · Earth

    The Moon forms

    Probably from debris of a giant impact with the young Earth. The exact timing is debated.

    ●●○Strong evidenceChapter 2: A Planet Is Born

  3. About 4.4 billion years ago · Earth

    Oldest known Earth minerals

    Zircon crystals from Western Australia, with chemical hints of early crust and possibly liquid water.

    ●○○Active researchChapter 2: A Planet Is Born

  4. At least about 3.5 billion years ago · Life

    Earliest widely accepted evidence of life

    Microbial structures in Western Australia. Older claims exist but are debated.

    ●●○Strong evidenceChapter 3: The First Breath of Life

  5. About 2.4 billion years ago · Life

    Great Oxidation Event begins

    Oxygen from photosynthetic microbes begins accumulating in the atmosphere, over a long and uneven period.

    ●●○Strong evidenceChapter 3: The First Breath of Life

  6. About 539 million years ago onwards · Life

    Cambrian diversification

    Most major animal groups appear in the fossil record over tens of millions of years.

    ●●●Well establishedChapter 4: When Life Diversified

Complex life

  1. Devonian, roughly 390–380 million years ago · Life

    The first forests

    Plants with wood and roots form the first forests on land.

    ●●○Strong evidenceChapter 4: When Life Diversified

  2. About 375 million years ago · Life

    Fish with limb-like fins: Tiktaalik

    A transitional fossil showing stages in the evolution of four-limbed animals.

    ●●●Well establishedChapter 4: When Life Diversified

  3. About 252 million years ago · Life

    End-Permian mass extinction

    The largest known extinction, closely linked to massive volcanism in Siberia.

    ●●●Well establishedChapter 4: When Life Diversified

  4. About 230 million years ago · Life

    The first dinosaurs

    The earliest widely accepted dinosaurs, such as Herrerasaurus, date to around 230 million years ago. The group may have arisen earlier; some estimates reach about 245 million years.

    ●●○Strong evidenceChapter 5: The Age of Giants

  5. About 66 million years ago · Life

    Non-avian dinosaurs die out

    The Chicxulub impact triggers a mass extinction; birds and mammals are among the survivors.

    ●●●Well establishedChapter 5: The Age of Giants

  6. About 7–6 million years ago · Human evolution

    Earliest proposed hominins

    Fossils such as Sahelanthropus. Their place in the human lineage is debated.

    ◐DebatedChapter 6: The Human Story Begins

Human evolution

  1. About 3.3 million years ago · Human evolution

    Oldest known stone tools

    Stone tools from Lomekwi, Kenya, older than the earliest known fossils of Homo.

    ●●○Strong evidenceChapter 6: The Human Story Begins

  2. About 1 million years ago · Human evolution

    Evidence of fire use

    Burned material at Wonderwerk Cave, South Africa. When fire use became habitual is debated.

    ◐DebatedChapter 6: The Human Story Begins

  3. About 300,000 years ago · Human evolution

    Homo sapiens emerges in Africa

    Based on current fossil evidence, including Jebel Irhoud in Morocco.

    ●●○Strong evidenceChapter 6: The Human Story Begins

  4. Within roughly the last 60,000 years · Human evolution

    Major dispersal out of Africa

    The expansion that gave rise to most ancestry of people outside Africa; earlier dispersals also occurred.

    ●○○Active researchChapter 6: The Human Story Begins

  5. About 40,000 years ago · Human evolution

    Neanderthals disappear

    Neanderthals vanish from the fossil record, but their DNA lives on in many people today.

    ●●○Strong evidenceChapter 6: The Human Story Begins

Civilisation

  1. More than 11,000 years ago · Civilisation

    Göbekli Tepe monuments

    Monumental stone enclosures built by people still largely living as hunter-gatherers.

    ●●●Well establishedChapter 7: The Birth of Civilisation

  2. Roughly 12,000–10,000 years ago · Civilisation

    Farming begins in Southwest Asia

    One of several independent centres of domestication.

    ●●●Well establishedChapter 7: The Birth of Civilisation

  3. About 9,000 years ago · Civilisation

    Maize domestication begins in Mexico

    Teosinte is gradually transformed into maize over thousands of years.

    ●●○Strong evidenceChapter 7: The Birth of Civilisation

  4. Around 3100 BCE · Civilisation

    Earliest known writing, Mesopotamia

    Proto-cuneiform tablets from Uruk, largely administrative records. Writing may have begun somewhat earlier.

    ●●●Well establishedChapter 7: The Birth of Civilisation

  5. From around 2500 BCE · Civilisation

    Indus cities flourish

    Planned cities such as Mohenjo-daro, with drainage and standardised bricks.

    ●●●Well establishedChapter 7: The Birth of Civilisation

  6. Around 1800 BCE · Ideas, science and technology

    Babylonian mathematical tablet Plimpton 322

    A clay tablet listing numbers related to right triangles. Its purpose is debated.

    ◐DebatedChapter 8: The Age of Ideas

  7. Around 1200 BCE · Civilisation

    Oracle-bone writing in China

    Inscriptions from the late Shang capital at Yinxu: the earliest substantial Chinese writing.

    ●●●Well establishedChapter 7: The Birth of Civilisation

  8. From the late 700s CE · Ideas, science and technology

    Translation movement in Baghdad

    Greek, Persian and Indian works are translated into Arabic and extended.

    ●●●Well establishedChapter 8: The Age of Ideas

  9. 868 CE · Ideas, science and technology

    Earliest complete dated printed book

    The Diamond Sutra, printed from woodblocks, found at Dunhuang in China.

    ●●●Well establishedChapter 8: The Age of Ideas

  10. 1450s CE · Ideas, science and technology

    Movable-type printing in Europe

    Gutenberg's press in Mainz helps printed books spread across Europe.

    ●●●Well establishedChapter 9: The World We Built

  11. 1610 CE · Ideas, science and technology

    Galileo's telescope observations

    Telescopic observations of the Moon and of four moons of Jupiter, published in The Starry Messenger.

    ●●●Well establishedChapter 9: The World We Built

  12. 1770s–1780s CE · Ideas, science and technology

    Watt's improved steam engines

    More efficient steam engines help power industrialisation.

    ●●●Well establishedChapter 9: The World We Built

  13. 1989 CE · Ideas, science and technology

    The World Wide Web is proposed

    Tim Berners-Lee proposes the Web at CERN.

    ●●●Well establishedChapter 9: The World We Built
